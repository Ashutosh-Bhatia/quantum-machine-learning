Quantum Computing Interactive Companion

A standalone interactive companion for your quantum computing lecture. It includes animated visualizations for single-qubit gates, measurement and projection, tensor products, multi-qubit controlled gates, Bell-state entanglement, oracle/query behavior, a classical-vs-quantum query comparison, and a step-by-step Deutsch algorithm explorer.

Modules

Companion overview

What this companion covers

The modules are aligned to the conceptual flow of a first lecture on quantum computing: qubits, quantum measurement, unitary gates, circuits, entanglement, query oracles, and Deutsch’s algorithm.

Visual intuition

See qubit states move on a Bloch-sphere style display, observe phase gates preserve measurement probabilities, and compare how different gates change the same input state.

Interactive experiments

Choose initial states, apply gates, build tensor products, run controlled gates, and simulate measurements using buttons and sliders.

Algorithmic understanding

Move from gate intuition to oracle-based computation and finally step through Deutsch’s algorithm one stage at a time.

Notes

Module 1

Single-Qubit Gate Lab

Bloch coordinates
x=0.00

Why this matters

X, Y, and Z are Pauli gates. H creates and removes superposition. S and T are phase gates. Use the same initial state with different gates and compare the outcomes.

Key lesson

Some gates change both amplitudes and probabilities, while phase gates may change relative phase without immediately changing measurement probabilities in the computational basis.

Module 2

Measurement & Projection

35°

What to observe

The projections onto the |0⟩ and |1⟩ axes determine the amplitudes. Squaring their magnitudes gives the measurement probabilities.

Teaching cue

Pause before clicking Measure. Ask students to predict the outcome probabilities. Then click several times and compare observed frequencies with the displayed probabilities.

After measurement, the state collapses to the observed basis state. The displayed “post-measurement state” reflects this collapse.
Module 3

Tensor Product Builder

Chosen single-qubit states

Tensor product

Dimension insight

A two-qubit state has 4 basis states: |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩.

Interpretation

This module helps students see how two single-qubit vectors become one four-dimensional joint state. It is especially useful before controlled gates and entanglement.

Suggested prompt in class

Ask: “If both qubits are in |+⟩, what are the amplitudes of all four basis states?” Then verify it here.

Module 5

Bell State & Entanglement

Circuit steps

  1. Start in |00⟩
  2. Apply H on qubit 1
  3. Apply CNOT
Current step: Start

Joint state

Entanglement insight

At the start, both qubits are independent.

Key message

After H and CNOT, the result is the Bell state \((|00⟩+|11⟩)/\sqrt2\), which cannot be written as a product of two single-qubit states.

Measurement behavior

Measuring the Bell state gives only correlated outcomes: 00 or 11, each with probability 1/2.
