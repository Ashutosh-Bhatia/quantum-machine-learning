What would change in a machine-learning model if part of its computation were performed by a quantum circuit? How would we represent the data, train the model, and decide whether the quantum component helped? These are the questions that motivate this book.

Why quantum machine learning?

Quantum machine learning brings ideas from quantum computing into the study and design of learning methods. A model may encode data into quantum states, use a parameterized circuit to transform those states, and form a prediction from measurements. Those choices raise familiar learning questions in a new computational setting.

The presence of a quantum circuit does not settle whether a model is useful. We still need a clear task, suitable data, a training procedure and a fair comparison. Our aim is to understand the complete process well enough to build small experiments and evaluate what they show.

Keep this learning loop in view as we develop the foundations. A state representation will become an encoding choice. A gate parameter will become something we can train. A measurement will become part of a prediction. The mathematics has a purpose in the model we eventually want to understand.

Who is this book for?

These notes are for quantum-computing and machine-learning enthusiasts, students, software developers, and professionals who want to understand QML and begin experimenting with it. If you already work with machine learning, you will have familiar ideas to build upon. If you are exploring the subject for the first time, the examples and prerequisite explanations provide a route into it.

A prior course in quantum physics is not required. We approach the subject through information, computation and learning. Basic algebra and elementary probability will help you begin. We introduce matrix, complex-number and further mathematical tools near their use. Familiarity with Python and basic ML will help with the programming and later chapters.

My aim is to help you move from curiosity to understanding: to explain a quantum learning model, reproduce a small experiment, and ask informed questions about its usefulness and limitations. The emphasis is on a careful foundation for exploration and professional judgment.

Why begin with quantum computing?

Our destination is quantum machine learning. To reach it, we need to understand the tools from which quantum learning models are built. Before constructing a quantum classifier, we need to know what a state represents, how a circuit changes it and what measurement allows us to observe.

A software developer can work with a computer through a computational model without first designing its transistors. We will use a similar idea of abstraction. The relevant physical principles will be explained, while the state, circuit and measurement descriptions give us a precise language for reasoning.

We will not skip the mathematics that makes these descriptions meaningful. Definitions, derivations and worked examples will show how each result follows. Familiar analogies help us enter a topic; we will also explain where those analogies stop being sufficient.

The three-part route

Part A — Quantum Computing Foundations. We begin with a conceptual introduction, then build the language of states, operations, measurement and circuits. Selected algorithms show how quantum features enter a computation.

Part B — The Classical Machine Learning Bridge. We revisit learning, optimization, feature representations, kernels and evaluation. This connects the quantum foundations with the learning methods we want to build.

Part C — Quantum Machine Learning. We bring the pieces together through encoding, parameterized circuits, quantum classifiers and quantum kernels, followed by training and careful evaluation against suitable alternatives.

How to learn with these notes

The written explanations and worked reasoning form the main path. When a question appears, pause and try the next step before revealing its answer. Interactive visualizations let you change a setting and examine its consequences. Quizzes explain the reasoning behind each choice, and exercises encourage you to work independently.

Academic figures sit beside the concepts they explain. You can pan a wide figure on a narrow screen. Read each figure together with its caption and the surrounding explanation.

Where programming helps, Python and Qiskit notebooks connect the theory to experiments. Colab links let you open the available companions; quantum-circuit programming will enter after the necessary concepts. Predict a result, calculate what you can by hand, run the experiment and explain what you observe.

Throughout the book we will return to the original question: how does the quantum component contribute to learning, what does the evidence show, and when is that contribution useful?
