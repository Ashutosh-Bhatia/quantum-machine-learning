Two qubits require a description of the joint system. We need to know not only what each individual measurement can return, but also how their outcomes occur together.

1. Declare the order before calculating

Call the subsystems A and B. Throughout this chapter, write |AB⟩ with A on the left. The ordered computational basis is |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, |11⟩. A joint pure state is

\[|\psi\rangle=a|00\rangle+b|01\rangle+c|10\rangle+d|11\rangle,\qquad |a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+|d|^2=1.\]

The column is (a,b,c,d)ᵀ. Four complex amplitudes do not mean that a single measurement returns four numbers. One joint Z measurement records one pair of bits. The squared amplitude of each basis vector gives that pair's probability.

In our notebook A=q1 and B=q0. Qiskit writes two-qubit state labels as |q1 q0⟩, so its amplitude list agrees with our |AB⟩ order. The top circuit wire is usually q0: wire height is not the same thing as the left-to-right order of the ket. We will keep this mapping visible rather than silently swapping conventions.

2. Independent pure preparations

If A is prepared as α|0⟩+β|1⟩ and B as γ|0⟩+δ|1⟩, their joint state is the tensor product

\[(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)\otimes(\gamma|0\rangle+\delta|1\rangle)=\alpha\gamma|00\rangle+\alpha\delta|01\rangle+\beta\gamma|10\rangle+\beta\delta|11\rangle.\]

For |+⟩A⊗|1⟩B, the joint column is (0,1/√2,0,1/√2)ᵀ. Only 01 and 11 occur under Z readout. B is certainly 1; A is evenly distributed. Reversing the factors gives a different column, so |+⟩⊗|1⟩ must not be confused with |1⟩⊗|+⟩.

3. Joint records and individual predictions

For the general column above, the joint Z probabilities are (|a|²,|b|²,|c|²,|d|²). To predict only A, sum over B:

\[P_A(0)=|a|^2+|b|^2,\quad P_A(1)=|c|^2+|d|^2.\]

Likewise PB(0)=|a|²+|c|² and PB(1)=|b|²+|d|². These are marginal probabilities, not a rule for obtaining a local pure-state amplitude by adding a and b. Distinct outcomes on B are mutually exclusive records, so their probabilities are added.

For n qubits the joint pure column has 2ⁿ entries. This describes the size of the mathematical state space; it does not give a method to read all entries or guarantee a computational advantage.

Practice and explain

  1. Expand |1⟩⊗|+⟩.
    Hint

    Keep A on the left.

    Worked solution

    The state is (|10⟩+|11⟩)/√2, column (0,0,1/√2,1/√2).

  2. For (1, i, 1, −1)/2, calculate both Z marginals.
    Hint

    Every joint probability is 1/4.

    Worked solution

    A and B each have probabilities (1/2,1/2). The phases do not enter this Z calculation.

  3. How many complex amplitudes describe a three-qubit pure column? Does one shot reveal them?
    Hint

    Count computational basis strings.

    Worked solution

    There are eight amplitudes. A joint Z shot returns one three-bit record, not the amplitude list.
