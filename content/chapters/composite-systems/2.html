Not every joint pure state can be assembled from a state for A and a state for B. Entanglement is precisely this failure of factorization for pure states.

1. Can the four entries be factored?

A product state has coefficients a=αγ, b=αδ, c=βγ, d=βδ. Therefore ad−bc=0. Conversely, for a nonzero normalized two-qubit pure state, this condition is sufficient: the coefficient matrix

\[C=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\]

has rank one when its determinant is zero and can be written as an outer product of two columns (without complex conjugating the second column). The factors can then be rescaled into normalized single-qubit vectors. This determinant criterion is restricted here to pure states of two qubits.

For plus⊗plus, all four entries are 1/2, so ad−bc=0. Each qubit is in a superposition, but their joint pure state is not entangled. Superposition and entanglement are different concepts.

2. The Bell state cannot be factored

\[|\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}.\]

Its coefficient matrix is diagonal with entries 1/√2. Its determinant is 1/2, not zero. A direct contradiction gives the same result: producing nonzero 00 and 11 amplitudes requires α,β,γ,δ all nonzero, which would also produce nonzero 01 and 10 amplitudes. Those amplitudes are absent in the Bell state.

For the normalized family cos(θ)|00⟩+eiφsin(θ)|11⟩ with 0≤θ≤π/2, the determinant is eiφcos(θ)sin(θ). The endpoints are product states; every interior point is entangled. At θ=π/4 the two Z outcomes are equally likely. Changing φ can change other-basis correlations without changing this Z distribution.

3. A joint pure state need not give local pure states

Bell-state Z marginals are uniform. That does not mean each qubit is in plus: plus has a certain X-positive outcome, whereas either Bell subsystem alone gives uniform X outcomes too. No single-qubit pure state reproduces all the local predictions of either Bell subsystem.

A density operator provides the general local description; its systematic treatment comes later. For now, calculate local probabilities by summing the joint predictions in the chosen basis. Do not assign separate local kets to an entangled pure state merely because each individual measurement has simple probabilities.

The determinant test assumes a pure-state model. Observing a correlated Z histogram is not enough to justify that assumption or certify entanglement: a classical random choice between 00 and 11 has the same Z distribution.

Practice and explain

  1. Test (1,1,−1,−1)/2 for entanglement and factor it.
    Hint

    Calculate ad−bc and compare plus/minus products.

    Worked solution

    The determinant is zero. It is minus on A tensor plus on B.

  2. Test (1,0,0,i)/√2.
    Hint

    Retain the complex phase in ad.

    Worked solution

    The determinant is i/2, so the pure state is entangled.

  3. Why does uniform local Z readout not prove each qubit is plus?
    Hint

    Ask what an X measurement predicts.

    Worked solution

    Uniform Z readout alone does not fix phase or purity. A Bell subsystem also has uniform X outcomes, whereas plus gives X-positive certainly.
