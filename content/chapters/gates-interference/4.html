A phase can be invisible in one measurement and decisive after a later gate. A three-gate experiment lets us see exactly where the difference enters.

1. Prepare, shift phase, recombine

Start from basis state 0. Apply H, then the phase gate P(φ), then H again. Time runs left to right in the circuit, while the output column is HP(φ)H|0⟩.

\[P(\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&e^{i\phi}\end{pmatrix}.\]

The intermediate state is (|0⟩+e^{iφ}|1⟩)/√2. Its Z probabilities are always 1/2 and 1/2. The second H combines these amplitudes:

\[HP(\phi)H|0\rangle=\frac{1+e^{i\phi}}2|0\rangle+\frac{1-e^{i\phi}}2|1\rangle.\]\[P(0)=\frac{1+\cos\phi}{2}=\cos^2(\phi/2),\qquad P(1)=\frac{1-\cos\phi}{2}=\sin^2(\phi/2).\]

At φ=0 the circuit returns 0 certainly. At φ=π it returns 1 certainly. At φ=π/2 the outputs are equally likely. No qubit has been copied and no complete statevector is returned by readout; a single execution still gives one classical outcome.

2. Where interference appears in the algebra

For two complex contributions a and b to the same output,

\[|a+b|^2=|a|^2+|b|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(a^*b).\]

The last term depends on their phase relation. Positive values reinforce the probability; negative values reduce it. The separate contributions are not mutually exclusive measured events, so adding their individual probabilities is not the correct calculation for the coherent experiment.

Here the contributions to output 0 are a=1/2 and b=e^{iφ}/2. Their individual squared magnitudes sum to 1/2, and the cross term is cos(φ)/2. Interference changes the distribution while the total probability over both outputs remains one.

If Z measurement is inserted just after the first H, then each branch enters the remaining phase gate as a basis state. That gate changes only a global phase on each branch, and final H gives a uniform Z distribution. Averaging these branches removes the phase-dependent fringe. This is a comparison of two different procedures, not an explanation that classical ignorance alone creates coherent phase.

  1. Calculate the final probabilities at φ=π/3.
    Hint

    Use cos(π/3)=1/2.

    Worked solution

    P(0)=3/4 and P(1)=1/4.

  2. For 200 fresh runs at φ=π/3, give expected counts and explain their meaning.
    Hint

    Multiply probabilities by 200.

    Worked solution

    Expected counts are 150 and 50. A sampled batch need not equal them exactly.

  3. Would a common phase e^{iγ} before the final H change this fringe?
    Hint

    Use linearity of H.

    Worked solution

    No. It multiplies both final amplitudes by the same unit-modulus factor and leaves probabilities unchanged.
