How do we transform a qubit so that the information we need becomes visible in a measurement? This chapter builds that connection through explicit state calculations, geometric intuition and controlled experiments.

From coherent evolution to readout

We begin with unitary action, learn the Pauli and Hadamard gates, then use a phase-shift-and-recombine experiment to derive interference. Parameterized rotations introduce the adjustable angles used later in QML. The final lesson connects circuit order and measurement basis.

Keep three descriptions together: the complex state column, its Bloch point, and the probabilities for a stated readout. Equal probabilities in one basis do not imply equal states. Unless explicitly stated, all circuits here are ideal and contain no intervening measurement.

How to work through this chapter

Read the derivations, predict each transformation, then use the six activities to test a choice of input, gate, angle or readout. Figures emphasize the calculation or procedure. Twelve concept checks and eighteen worked practice problems support self-assessment.
