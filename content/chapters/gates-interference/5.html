Fixed gates are useful landmarks. Parameterized gates let us move continuously between states, which is essential when a circuit encodes data or adjusts trainable parameters.

1. S and T are particular phase shifts

\[S=P(\pi/2)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&i\end{pmatrix},\qquad T=P(\pi/4)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&e^{i\pi/4}\end{pmatrix}.\]

They leave the magnitude of each component unchanged, so neither changes immediate Z probabilities. They can change later readout after a mixing gate. S maps plus to (|0⟩+i|1⟩)/√2. T maps plus to an equatorial state with azimuth π/4. Their powers satisfy

\[T^2=S,\quad S^2=Z,\quad T^4=Z,\quad S^4=T^8=I.\]

The inverse phase gate is P(−φ)=P(φ)†. S and T are not their own inverses. Distinguish exact matrix identities from identities that hold only up to global phase.

2. Rotations use half-angles in their matrices

For the Pauli matrix A equal to X, Y or Z, define RA(θ)=exp(−iθA/2). Since A²=I, separating the even and odd powers in the exponential gives cos(θ/2)I−i sin(θ/2)A. Thus, with c=cos(θ/2) and s=sin(θ/2),

\[R_x(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}c&-is\\-is&c\end{pmatrix},\quad R_y(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}c&-s\\s&c\end{pmatrix},\quad R_z(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta/2}&0\\0&e^{i\theta/2}\end{pmatrix}.\]

The physical Bloch rotation angle is θ, not θ/2. Positive rotations follow the right-hand rule about the named axis with the Bloch coordinates defined in Chapter 4. In particular, Ry(θ)|0⟩=cos(θ/2)|0⟩+sin(θ/2)|1⟩ and Rx(θ)|0⟩=cos(θ/2)|0⟩−i sin(θ/2)|1⟩. At θ=π/2, their Bloch points are +x and −y respectively.

Rz(φ)=e−iφ/2P(φ). For an isolated single-qubit transformation, they give globally equivalent outputs and identical measurement probabilities. Likewise Rx(π)=−iX. A Pauli half-turn and the corresponding rotation have the same Bloch action but different exact matrices. In larger controlled constructions a phase relation must be tracked carefully; global equivalence of a single-qubit gate is not permission to discard a relative phase between controlled branches.

3. From an angle to a learning parameter

For θ=2π/3, Ry(θ)|0⟩=(1/2,√3/2)ᵀ, so Z probabilities are (1/4,3/4). This already provides a simple data-encoding map: choose θ from a real feature, prepare the state, and specify the measurement used to obtain a prediction. A trainable angle uses the same mathematics, but its value is adjusted by an optimization procedure introduced later.

Angles in the equations and Qiskit are in radians. The activity labels its controls in degrees for geometric intuition and converts them internally. Rotation by 2π gives −I at the statevector level; it returns the same physical pure state. Rotation by 4π gives I exactly.

Predict before calculating. Use the experiment or notebook to check your reasoning.

  1. Calculate S† and T†.
    Hint

    Conjugate their diagonal phases.

    Worked solution

    S†=diag(1,−i); T†=diag(1,exp(−iπ/4)).

  2. Find Ry(π)|0⟩ and Rx(π)|0⟩.
    Hint

    Use cosine zero and sine one at the half-angle.

    Worked solution

    The outputs are |1⟩ and −i|1⟩. They are physically equivalent pure states, though their columns differ.

  3. Why does Rz on basis state 0 not change its Bloch point?
    Hint

    Only one component is nonzero.

    Worked solution

    It multiplies zero by exp(−iθ/2), a global phase. The north-pole Bloch point remains fixed.
