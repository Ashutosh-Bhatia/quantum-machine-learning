The Pauli gates are a small collection with different effects. Their matrices make those effects precise, while the Bloch sphere gives us a complementary geometric view.

1. Four matrices worth knowing

\[I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.\]

For ψ=(α,β)ᵀ, these give Iψ=(α,β)ᵀ, Xψ=(β,α)ᵀ, Yψ=(−iβ,iα)ᵀ and Zψ=(α,−β)ᵀ. X swaps components; Z changes their relative sign when both are nonzero. Y combines swapping with phase factors. Identity is included as the operation that leaves the vector unchanged; X, Y and Z are the three nonidentity Pauli matrices.

All four are unitary and Hermitian. Each squares to I, so each is its own inverse. Hermitian and unitary are different properties: the phase gates introduced later are unitary without generally being Hermitian.

For ψ=(√3/2,i/2)ᵀ, Xψ=(i/2,√3/2)ᵀ, Yψ=(1/2,i√3/2)ᵀ and Zψ=(√3/2,−i/2)ᵀ. X and Y exchange the Z probabilities from (3/4,1/4) to (1/4,3/4); Z preserves them.

2. A sign change depends on the input

Z|1⟩=−|1⟩ differs from |1⟩ only by a global phase. But Z|+⟩=|−⟩ changes the relative sign of two nonzero components. The same gate can leave one input physically unchanged and change another input observably. Likewise X|+⟩=|+⟩ and X|−⟩=−|−⟩: swapping components does not always change the represented state.

On the Bloch sphere, the coordinate transformations are

\[X:(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-y,-z),\quad Y:(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,y,-z),\quad Z:(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,-y,z).\]

These are half-turns about the named axes. Points on the rotation axis stay fixed as physical states. The statevector may still acquire a global phase. We use the sphere to map pure-state transformations, not to claim that a physical particle follows this spatial path.

Practice and explain

Predict before calculating. Use the experiment or notebook to check your reasoning.

  1. Calculate Y|0⟩ and Y|1⟩.
    Hint

    Use the columns of Y.

    Worked solution

    They are i|1⟩ and −i|0⟩. Each is globally equivalent to the corresponding flipped basis state.

  2. Verify Y²=I including its phases.
    Hint

    Apply Y to each basis vector twice.

    Worked solution

    Y(i|1⟩)=i(−i)|0⟩=|0⟩, and Y(−i|0⟩)=−i(i)|1⟩=|1⟩.

  3. Starting from plus, compare Z probabilities before and after Z, then X probabilities.
    Hint

    Z sends plus to minus.

    Worked solution

    Z probabilities remain (1/2,1/2). X changes from (1,0) to (0,1).
