A state describes a preparation. A gate describes a deliberate transformation of that state. Before learning a catalogue of gates, we need to know what makes their action consistent and how to calculate it.

1. Multiply the column, then interpret it

In the computational basis, an ideal single-qubit gate is a 2 × 2 unitary matrix U. For a normalized pure input, its action is

\[|\psi'\rangle=U|\psi\rangle,\qquad U=\begin{pmatrix}u_{00}&u_{01}\\u_{10}&u_{11}\end{pmatrix},\qquad |\psi\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\\beta\end{pmatrix}.\]\[\alpha'=u_{00}\alpha+u_{01}\beta,\qquad\beta'=u_{10}\alpha+u_{11}\beta.\]

Each output amplitude combines contributions from both input components. Probabilities are calculated only after these complex contributions are added. A gate is not a measurement: applying U does not select one of the basis states or produce an outcome record.

The columns of U are U|0⟩ and U|1⟩. Once these two actions are known, linearity fixes its action on every superposition: U(α|0⟩+β|1⟩)=αU|0⟩+βU|1⟩. Do not replace a superposition by a random choice between those inputs.

2. Why unitary?

The condition U†U=I has two immediate consequences. First, it preserves normalization:

\[\langle\psi'|\psi'\rangle=\langle\psi|U^\dagger U|\psi\rangle=\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1.\]

Second, U† is the inverse. Applying it after U recovers the original vector. There is no conditional renormalization after a valid unitary gate. A nonunitary matrix cannot be turned into a deterministic closed-system gate simply by normalizing its output; that would generally describe a different operation.

Unitary evolution also preserves inner products, not just individual norms. It cannot send two distinct non-orthogonal states to perfectly distinguishable orthogonal ones. This connects gate evolution to the discrimination and no-cloning limitations in Chapter 4.

Our model assumes ideal coherent control of one qubit. Measurement, reset and noisy evolution need a broader description; a circuit may contain such operations, but they are not unitary gates.

Practice and explain

Predict before calculating. Use the experiment or notebook to check your reasoning.

  1. For U=diag(1,i), transform (√3/2,1/2).
    Hint

    Multiply each diagonal entry by its component.

    Worked solution

    The output is (√3/2,i/2). Its squared norm remains 3/4+1/4=1.

  2. Why is diag(1,0) not a deterministic unitary gate?
    Hint

    Apply it to basis state 1.

    Worked solution

    It maps a normalized input to the zero vector and has no inverse. It is a measurement projector, not a unitary.

  3. Show that a unitary preserves an inner product.
    Hint

    Insert U†U between the bra and ket.

    Worked solution

    ⟨Uu|Uv⟩=⟨u|U†U|v⟩=⟨u|v⟩.
