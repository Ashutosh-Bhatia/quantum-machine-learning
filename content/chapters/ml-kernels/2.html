Support vector machines connect classification to geometric margins. Their kernel form shows why pairwise inner products matter for the later quantum bridge.

A separator with a scale convention

For labels yi∈{−1,+1}, a linear separator predicts the sign of wᵀx+b. Rescaling w and b leaves the boundary unchanged, so constraints fix a useful convention:

\[\min_{w,b}\frac12\|w\|^2\quad\text{subject to}\quad y_i(w^Tx_i+b)\ge1.\]

This hard-margin formulation assumes separability. The two supporting hyperplanes are wᵀx+b=±1 and their distance is 2/||w||. Points on the margin can determine the solution. Soft-margin SVMs add nonnegative slack variables and a penalty C for violations, allowing mislabeled or overlapping classes.

The soft-margin objective is ½||w||²+C∑ξi. Larger C penalizes violations more strongly, while smaller C allows more violations in exchange for a smaller weight norm. This convention differs from a λ penalty directly added to a mean loss.

The dual exposes inner products

\[\max_\alpha\sum_i\alpha_i-\frac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_jy_iy_jk(x_i,x_j),\quad0\le\alpha_i\le C,\quad\sum_i\alpha_iy_i=0.\]

The decision score is ∑i αi yi k(xi,x)+b. Examples with nonzero αi are support vectors. Replacing a dot product by a valid kernel permits nonlinear boundaries in the original input space. The score's sign gives the binary decision; the score is not automatically a calibrated probability.

Kernel and hyperparameter choices

\[k_{\mathrm{RBF}}(x,z)=\exp(-\gamma\|x-z\|^2),\qquad\gamma>0.\]

A larger γ makes similarity decay over shorter distances. Its effect depends on feature scaling and the data. Tune C and γ within validation, keeping preprocessing inside the folds. How the α values build the boundary

The dual coefficients weight labelled training vectors: \(w=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i\). Changing them changes the normal vector and therefore the boundary orientation. Feasible coefficients must satisfy \(0\le\alpha_i\le C\) and \(\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0\). These constraints alone do not establish optimality.

For the four points below, linked pairs a=α₀=α₃ and b=α₁=α₂ preserve the equality constraint. They give w=(2(a+b),2(a−b)). The dual objective reduces to 2(a+b)−4(a²+b²), maximized at a=b=1/4. With intercept zero, every point has functional margin one and the primal and dual objectives both equal 1/2.

Try making one pair zero: the boundary rotates and some points violate the margin. Then animate ascent. At the optimum all four points are support vectors in this deliberately symmetric dataset. In a general dataset many α values may be zero. A nonzero dual coefficient and a visible point near a boundary should not be confused with a proof that an arbitrary hand-adjusted model satisfies the KKT conditions.

    Worked solution

    w=(1,1). Some points have functional margin zero, while others have margin two. The dual objective is zero, below the optimum 1/2, and the primal-dual gap is positive. Equality and box constraints establish feasibility, not optimality.
