A model needs a numerical definition of error before it can be trained. An optimizer changes parameters to improve that objective; it does not decide whether the objective is appropriate.

From a residual to an empirical objective

\[\hat y_i=wx_i+b,\quad r_i=\hat y_i-y_i,\quad L(w,b)=\frac1N\sum_i r_i^2.\]

Mean squared error penalizes large residuals strongly and has the squared units of the target. Mean absolute error averages |r| and retains the original units. For residuals 1 and −3, MSE is 5 and MAE is 2. A residual sign indicates direction, while its squared magnitude measures one contribution to MSE.

For binary classification, predicted probabilities and hard labels are different outputs. A probability model can be trained with binary cross-entropy, −[y log p+(1−y)log(1−p)], while a threshold converts probabilities into decisions. Accuracy alone does not measure probability quality, and choosing an application threshold is not the same as fitting the model.

What does one training step do?

\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}=\frac2N\sum_i r_ix_i,\qquad\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}=\frac2N\sum_i r_i,\qquad\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\nabla L(\theta_t).\]

The learning rate η controls the step size. Both derivatives must be evaluated at the same current parameters before updating them. A large learning rate can increase the loss or cause divergence; an extremely small one can make progress slow. Gradient descent is a procedure, not a guarantee that every objective will be solved globally.

The loop is predict, calculate loss, compute a gradient, update, and repeat. Training data drives those parameter updates. Validation data guides choices such as regularization strength or model family. The final test data is reserved for evaluation after those choices.

Keep the objective honest

Optimizing training loss does not directly optimize unknown future performance. A constant predictor can serve as a baseline, and the evaluation metric should reflect the task. A QML circuit later supplies another parameterized prediction function; the purpose of the classical training loop remains recognizable.
