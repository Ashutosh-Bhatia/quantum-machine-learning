A two-entry vector and another two-entry vector cannot describe a joint system by simply being added. To combine subsystem descriptions we need a product that keeps track of every pair of basis labels.

1. Expand in a declared order

For columns u=(a,b) and v=(c,d), define their tensor product by blocks:

\[u\otimes v=\begin{pmatrix}av\\bv\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}ac\\ad\\bc\\bd\end{pmatrix}.\]

The coordinate order is (00,01,10,11), with the first label from u and the second from v. If u has m entries and v has n, the product has mn entries. This is not an inner product, outer product or concatenation. With u=(1,2) and v=(3,4), the answer is (3,4,6,8), rather than (1,2,3,4).

In ket notation, \(|a\rangle\otimes|b\rangle=|ab\rangle\). Bilinearity lets us expand:

\[|+\rangle\otimes\frac{|0\rangle+i|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}=\frac{|00\rangle+i|01\rangle+|10\rangle+i|11\rangle}{2}.\]

Every pair of terms contributes its product of coefficients. The squared norm factors:

\[\|u\otimes v\|^2=\sum_{j,k}|u_jv_k|^2=\left(\sum_j|u_j|^2\right)\left(\sum_k|v_k|^2\right).\]

Thus normalized subsystem vectors give a normalized joint product vector. Not every normalized joint vector factors: the Bell-state example introduced in Chapter 1 is a counterexample. We will study separability and entanglement systematically in the composite-systems chapter.

2. Tensor products of operators

If A acts on the first subsystem and B on the second, their joint action satisfies

\[(A\otimes B)(u\otimes v)=(Au)\otimes(Bv).\]

In coordinates, each entry of A multiplies the whole block B. For a 2×2 A,

\[A\otimes B=\begin{pmatrix}A_{00}B&A_{01}B\\A_{10}B&A_{11}B\end{pmatrix}.\]

For two qubits, \(H\otimes I\) applies H to the first ket label, whereas \(I\otimes H\) applies it to the second. In the declared order, \((H\otimes I)|01\rangle=(|01\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\). We transformed the first label, leaving the second equal to 1.

Swapping factors generally changes the coordinate array: u⊗v need not equal v⊗u. The systems can be related by an explicit swap, but silently exchanging them is a convention error. Programming libraries may display circuit wires and ket strings differently; declare the ordering before comparing a program with a hand calculation.

Identifying the type and shape before calculating is often the quickest error check. These operations all multiply entries, but their index rules and purposes differ.

Practice and explain

Attempt each calculation before opening its solution. Keep intermediate steps and state the convention you use.

  1. Expand. Compute \((1,-1)^{\mathsf T}\otimes(1,i)^{\mathsf T}/2\).
    Hint

    Multiply every coefficient of the first factor by the entire second factor.

    Worked solution

    The entries are \((1,i,-1,-i)^{\mathsf T}/2\). Their squared magnitudes sum to one.

  2. Track the label. In order (00,01,10,11), write \(|1\rangle\otimes|0\rangle\).
    Hint

    Find the location of 10 in the declared order.

    Worked solution

    It is \(|10\rangle=(0,0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\).

  3. Act on the second system. Find \((I\otimes H)|01\rangle\).
    Hint

    Only the second ket label is changed.

    Worked solution

    It is \(|0\rangle\otimes H|1\rangle=(|00\rangle-|01\rangle)/\sqrt2\).
