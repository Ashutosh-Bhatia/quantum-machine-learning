Changing the order of a row and column changes the kind of answer. A bra times a ket gives a scalar. A ket times a bra gives a matrix. That matrix can act on another vector.

1. Construct an outer product

\[|u\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}1\\i\end{pmatrix},\quad \langle v|=\begin{pmatrix}2&-i\end{pmatrix},\quad |u\rangle\langle v|=\begin{pmatrix}2&-i\\2i&1\end{pmatrix}.\]

Each column is u multiplied by the corresponding entry of the bra. The bottom-right entry is \(i(-i)=1\). In general, u with m entries and v with n entries give an m×n matrix with entry \(u_jv_k^*\). The notation \(|u\rangle\langle v|\) includes conjugation through the bra; do not conjugate its entries a second time.

When this matrix acts on w, associativity gives \((|u\rangle\langle v|)|w\rangle=|u\rangle(\langle v|w\rangle)\). The inner product on the right is one scalar, which scales u. This is a useful way to understand what a rank-one operator does.

2. Projection onto a unit direction

For a normalized vector u, define \(P_u=|u\rangle\langle u|\). Acting on v gives the component along that direction:

\[P_u|v\rangle=\langle u|v\rangle|u\rangle,\qquad P_u^2=|u\rangle\underbrace{\langle u|u\rangle}_{1}\langle u|=P_u.\]

The second identity is idempotence: projecting an already projected vector changes nothing. A projector is also Hermitian, meaning \(P_u^\dagger=P_u\). These properties concern the linear projection; subsequent normalization of a measurement branch is a separate step.

Normalization of u matters. For a nonzero direction that is not unit length, the projector is \(P_u=uu^\dagger/(u^\dagger u)\). For u=(2,0), the raw outer product is diag(4,0), which is not idempotent. Dividing by 4 gives diag(1,0), the actual projector.

3. Projection, probability and a normalized branch

Let \(|\psi\rangle=(|0\rangle+\sqrt3|1\rangle)/2\) and \(P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\). There are three distinct calculations:

\[P_0|\psi\rangle=\tfrac12|0\rangle,\quad p_0=\|P_0|\psi\rangle\|^2=\tfrac14,\quad \frac{P_0|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{p_0}}=|0\rangle.\]

The first result is an unnormalized vector, the second a probability, and the third the state conditioned on that outcome. The last step is valid only if \(p_0>0\). A zero-probability branch cannot be normalized. We divide amplitudes by the square root of the probability, not by the probability itself.

For an orthonormal basis the projectors satisfy \(\sum_jP_j=I\). Every normalized input therefore has total outcome probability one. We develop the measurement rules more fully in Chapter 4; here we have established the algebra that makes them readable.

Practice and explain

Attempt each calculation before opening its solution. Keep intermediate steps and state the convention you use.

  1. Build the matrix. Compute \(|+\rangle\langle+|\).
    Hint

    Write plus as a column and its bra as a row.

    Worked solution

    It is \(\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\). Multiplying it by itself returns the same matrix.

  2. Keep the conjugate. Find \(uu^\dagger\) for \(u=(1,i)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2\).
    Hint

    The conjugate of i is −i.

    Worked solution

    The result is \(\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\i&1\end{pmatrix}\). It is Hermitian and idempotent.

  3. Condition correctly. For \(\psi=(\sqrt3/2,1/2)^{\mathsf T}\), project onto basis state 1 and normalize.
    Hint

    Separate the projected vector from its squared norm.

    Worked solution

    The projected vector is \((0,1/2)^{\mathsf T}\), with probability 1/4. Divide it by 1/2 to obtain \((0,1)^{\mathsf T}\).
