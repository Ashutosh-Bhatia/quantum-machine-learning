We already know how to label a classical state and apply a matrix to a column. This chapter supplies the mathematical language needed when the entries become complex amplitudes: magnitude and phase, inner products, projectors, unitary maps and tensor products.

Learn the calculation, then use it

We will not ask unfamiliar notation to explain itself. Each lesson starts with a concrete calculation, develops its general rule, and gives you a way to predict and inspect the result. Figures support the written reasoning; the activities do not replace it.

Begin with complex numbers, then build the real dot product and norm. Conjugation leads to complex inner products and bras. Outer products give projectors; matrices give transformations; unitaries identify norm-preserving gates; tensor products combine spaces. The same ideas return in Chapter 4’s state and measurement rules.

Basic algebra and Chapter 2’s columns are sufficient to begin. Trigonometric functions are introduced through angles; you can study the Cartesian calculations before exploring phase.

Practice and explain

Attempt each calculation before opening its solution. Keep intermediate steps and state the convention you use.

  1. A normalized complex state. For \(\psi=((1+2i)/3,-2/3)^{\mathsf T}\), calculate its norm, bra and computational-basis squared magnitudes.
    Hint

    Add the two squared magnitudes; conjugate each entry for the bra.

    Worked solution

    The squared norm is 5/9+4/9=1. Its bra is \(((1-2i)/3,-2/3)\); the squared magnitudes are 5/9 and 4/9.

  2. Two kinds of products. For \(u=(1,i)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2\), compute \(u^\dagger u\) and \(uu^\dagger\).
    Hint

    Keep track of the output dimensions before multiplying.

    Worked solution

    The first is the scalar 1. The second is \(\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\i&1\end{pmatrix}\), a projector. Check its square.

  3. A complex projector. For that u and v=(1,0), find Pv and its squared norm, where \(P=uu^\dagger\).
    Hint

    Apply the projector matrix to the basis column.

    Worked solution

    The output is (1,i)/2 with squared norm 1/2. Its normalized branch is u.

  4. An error to diagnose. A learner writes \(\|(1,i)\|^2=1+i^2=0\). Identify and repair the error.
    Hint

    Use conjugation in the self-product.

    Worked solution

    They used squares instead of squared magnitudes. The result must be \(1+|i|^2=2\).

  5. Combine and transform. Find \((H\otimes I)(|0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle)\) and check its norm.
    Hint

    Use (A⊗B)(u⊗v)=Au⊗Bv.

    Worked solution

    The output is \((|01\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\), whose squared norm is 1/2+1/2=1.

  6. Explain the scope. Why is a projector useful in quantum mathematics even though it is not usually unitary?
    Hint

    Distinguish component extraction from reversible evolution.

    Worked solution

    It extracts a component used in measurement calculations. A conditional branch and its probability require additional rules; it is not a reversible closed-system gate.
