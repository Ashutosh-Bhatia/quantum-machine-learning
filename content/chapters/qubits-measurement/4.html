The Born rule predicts the next outcome. A conditional-state rule tells us how to describe the system after that outcome is recorded. We must keep the projected column, its probability and its normalized branch separate.

1. Calculate three quantities, not one

For an ideal rank-one projective measurement with basis vector a, define \(P_a=|a\rangle\langle a|\). Then

\[\text{branch}=P_a|\psi\rangle,\qquad p_a=\|P_a|\psi\rangle\|^2,\qquad |\psi_a\rangle=\frac{P_a|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{p_a}}\quad(p_a>0).\]

The first object is an unnormalized vector, the second a scalar probability, and the third the state conditioned on that recorded outcome. We divide by the norm, which is the square root of the probability. If the probability is zero, that branch does not occur in this ideal model and has no conditional normalized state.

A real example

For \(\psi=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)^{\mathsf T}\), outcome 0 gives branch (1/2,0), probability 1/4 and normalized state (1,0). Outcome 1 has probability 3/4 and conditional state (0,1).

Keep a complex phase in the calculation

For the complex example from Lesson 4.2, the outcome-0 branch is \(((1+2i)/3,0)^{\mathsf T}\). Its probability is 5/9. Dividing by \(\sqrt5/3\) gives

\[|\psi_0\rangle=\frac{1+2i}{\sqrt5}|0\rangle.\]

The prefactor has magnitude one, so this vector is globally equivalent to basis state 0. Writing the raw normalized vector first makes clear why its remaining phase is not a new measurement prediction.

2. Remeasure, or prepare again?

After ideal Z outcome 0, an immediate ideal Z measurement gives 0 again. This assumes the same retained system, the same basis and no intervening operation or disturbance. It does not mean that preparing the original superposition again will give the same record.

Suppose the initial preparation is plus. If we prepare plus afresh before each Z measurement, each trial has probabilities 1/2 and 1/2. If instead we retain the qubit after its first Z measurement and immediately measure Z repeatedly, later records repeat that first outcome in this ideal model.

A different intervening measurement changes the situation. Start with basis state 0, measure X, and keep its outcome. Either + or − occurs with probability 1/2, leaving the corresponding X-basis state. A subsequent Z measurement then gives 0 or 1 with probability 1/2 even after we condition on the particular X record.

Projectors are not ordinary reversible unitary gates. They belong to a measurement description that includes the outcome probabilities and conditional normalization. We are using the ideal projective model here, not claiming that every physical measurement has this exact form.

Practice and explain

Predict first, calculate by hand, then compare your reasoning with the solution.

  1. For (1/2,√(3)/2), calculate the outcome-1 branch, probability and conditional state.
    Hint

    Apply diag(0,1), then divide by the branch norm.

    Worked solution

    Branch (0,√(3)/2), probability 3/4, conditional state (0,1).

  2. For the complex slide state, find the conditional vector after outcome 0 and compare it with |0>.
    Hint

    Retain the complex coefficient when normalizing.

    Worked solution

    The vector is ((1+2i)/√(5),0), differing from |0> only by a unit-modulus overall phase.

  3. From |0>, measure X and then Z. Give probabilities for both Z outcomes even after recording X-positive.
    Hint

    Use the plus state as the conditional X-positive state.

    Worked solution

    X-positive and X-negative each have probability 1/2. Conditioned on either, Z gives 0/1 each with probability 1/2.
