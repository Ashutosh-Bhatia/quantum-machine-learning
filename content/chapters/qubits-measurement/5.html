A pure qubit can be represented by a point on a sphere. The sphere is a map of states after removing overall phase, not the location of a particle and not a graph whose coordinates are the two amplitudes.

1. Why two angles suffice

Two complex amplitudes contain four real numbers. Normalization imposes one real constraint, and overall phase is physically irrelevant for the isolated pure state. The remaining two parameters can be written as

\[|\psi\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)|1\rangle,\qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi.\]

When α is nonzero, remove its phase to choose it real and nonnegative, then write its magnitude as cos(θ/2). The other magnitude follows from normalization. At α=0 this gauge choice does not fix the remaining overall phase; all azimuths at that pole represent the same physical state.

Define the Bloch coordinates by

\[(x,y,z)=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\ \sin\theta\sin\phi,\ \cos\theta).\]

They satisfy \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\). From general normalized amplitudes, the same coordinates are \(x=2\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^*\beta)\), \(y=2\operatorname{Im}(\alpha^*\beta)\), \(z=|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2\). A common phase cancels from these expressions.

The probabilities follow from the half-angle identities:

\[P_Z(0)=\cos^2(\theta/2)=\frac{1+z}{2},\qquad P_Z(1)=\frac{1-z}{2}.\]

Thus z is neither P(1) nor a probability amplitude. It can be negative. The X-positive probability from the previous lesson is \((1+x)/2\). The coordinate y records another part of the relative-phase information; other measurement directions will be developed with the gates and bases in Chapter 5.

2. Read the landmarks and a worked point

At θ=0, the north pole is basis state 0; at θ=π, the south pole is basis state 1. The equator has θ=π/2 and equal Z probabilities. There, φ=0 gives plus, φ=π gives minus, and φ=π/2 gives \((|0\rangle+i|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\). These points have different relative phases despite equal Z statistics.

For θ=2π/3 and φ=π/2, the state is \((1/2,i\sqrt3/2)^{\mathsf T}\). The point is \((0,\sqrt3/2,-1/2)\) and Z probabilities are 1/4 and 3/4. The phase φ is not halved in the exponential; the polar angle θ is halved in the amplitudes.

This explorer shows pure states on the surface. General mixed states require a richer description, introduced later. A zero arrow at the center must not be mistaken for a normalized pure state.

Practice and explain

Predict first, calculate by hand, then compare your reasoning with the solution.

  1. For θ=2π/3, φ=pi/2, find amplitudes, Bloch coordinates and Z probabilities.
    Hint

    Remember the half-angle in the amplitudes.

    Worked solution

    Amplitudes (1/2,i √(3)/2); coordinates (0,√(3)/2,−1/2); probabilities (1/4,3/4).

  2. Identify the points of plus, minus and plus-i.
    Hint

    Use θ=pi/2 for the equator.

    Worked solution

    Their points are (1,0,0), (−1,0,0) and (0,1,0).

  3. If PZ(1)=1/4, what is z, and is the whole Bloch point determined?
    Hint

    Solve PZ(1)=(1−z)/2.

    Worked solution

    z=1/2. The azimuth remains undetermined, so the full pure-state point is not fixed.
