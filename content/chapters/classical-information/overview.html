What does it mean to describe a system mathematically? Before introducing qubits, we build a complete classical model: states as labels, uncertainty as probability vectors, operations as stochastic matrices, and sequences as matrix products.

This chapter follows the systems-first approach in the lecture slides. The theory, proofs and worked examples are the main reading path. The small experiments help you test predictions; each remains understandable through the accompanying text and equations.

Before you begin

Basic arithmetic and elementary algebra are enough to start. We explain the column-vector convention and include a matrix-multiplication refresher in Lesson 1.4. No prior quantum physics or Qiskit experience is required.

By the end of this chapter

  • Choose a finite state set and interpret basis vectors in a stated order.
  • Distinguish model probabilities, empirical frequencies, and conditional probabilities.
  • Construct a stochastic matrix from conditional output rules and derive \(p'=Mp\).
  • Compose operations in the right order and prove that their product remains stochastic.

Lesson sequence

  1. Finite systems, states and basis labels

    Start with a bit, switch and die. Separate a state label, a coordinate vector and a binary encoding.

  2. Probability distributions and repeated observations

    Describe uncertainty, interpret finite samples and derive conditional probabilities.

  3. Deterministic and stochastic operations

    Build operation matrices, derive their action, and distinguish normalization from reversibility.

  4. Composing operations

    Track intermediate states, work a noncommuting example and prove stochastic closure.

How to study the chapter

Read each explanation and work the calculation before changing a control. The embedded checks give feedback for each answer. End-of-lesson exercises include hints and worked reasoning. The recorded check count is a browser-local study aid, not a mastery score or a restriction on navigation.
